# Chapter 17: The User Roles (Extended Example)

## Core Idea
The first stage of the worked example: identify, consolidate, and model the **user roles** for the South Coast Nautical Supplies online bookstore (a print-catalog sailing-supply company going online to sell books, live in 30 days).

## What This Demonstrates
- Applies the **user-role modeling** technique (Ch 3): brainstorm role cards, place them on a table, consolidate duplicates/overlaps, add role attributes, then optionally add **personas**.
- Shows the role of an internal **customer proxy** (Lori, VP Sales & Marketing) standing in for the real end users (sailors, who are all outside the company).

## Worked Example

**Initial brainstorm (role cards on the table):** Hardcore Sailor, Novice Sailor, New Sailor, Gift-Buyer, Non-Sailing Spouse, Administrator, Sales Vice President, Charter Captain, Experienced Sailor, Sailing School, Library, Instructor.

**Consolidation moves:**
- *New Sailor* merged into **Novice Sailor** (a Novice has sailed for years but not often enough to be good; not different enough to keep two).
- *Sailing School* torn up; kept **Instructor** (a discrete person; even a school admin shares Instructor traits).
- *Charter Captain* merged into **Hardcore Sailor** (essentially the same — knows exactly which book they want).
- *Gift-Buyer* + *Non-Sailing Spouse* → consolidated **Non-Sailing Gift Buyer**.
- *Sales Vice President* renamed to the more generic **Report Viewer** (non-purchasing, but CEO mandates watching sales).
- *Library* → renamed **Librarian** (roles should represent discrete users).
- **Administrator** kept (first non-purchasing role: loads data, keeps system running).

**Final 8 roles (with modeled attributes):**
- **Novice Sailor** — experienced web shopper; ~6 purchases in first 3 months; needs help choosing books.
- **Instructor** — uses site weekly; places bulk repeat orders (e.g., 20 copies); nervous around computers; price-focused; no interest in "frills."
- **Hardcore Sailor** — inexperienced with computers; big catalog buyer but few books; knows exactly what he wants; doesn't want to feel stupid.
- **Experienced Sailor** — computer-proficient; orders 1–2×/quarter; cares about other sailors' opinions/best products.
- **Non-Sailing Gift Buyer** — computer-proficient; not a sailor; usually after a specific book or topic.
- **Librarian** — computer-proficient; orders by ISBN; no frills; few but large orders/year.
- **Administrator** — highly proficient; daily back-end use; wants shortcuts later.
- **Report Viewer** — moderate (spreadsheet-level) skill; wants deep data; trades speed for power.

**Personas added** (because Instructors and Experienced Sailors are critical to satisfy):
- **Teresa** — sailing 4 years; biotech CEO; comfortable ordering online; sails only spring/summer; busy, wants speed; married to Tom (non-sailor).
- **Captain Ron** — sailing 40 years; runs a San Diego sailing school; 10-year loyal catalog customer; intimidated by computers but willing to try.

> Caveat from the book: for a system this small, personas are arguably unnecessary; Teresa and Captain Ron are included to make the example complete.

## Role-Modeling Factors Used
1. Frequency of use
2. Domain expertise
3. General computer/software proficiency
4. Proficiency with *this* software
5. General goal (convenience vs. rich experience, etc.)

## Key Takeaways
1. Start wide (brainstorm every conceivable role), then aggressively consolidate overlaps and duplicates so you end with a small, discrete set.
2. Keep non-purchasing roles (Administrator, Report Viewer) — they generate real stories too.
3. Rename roles toward discrete individuals (Library → Librarian) and generic intent (Sales VP → Report Viewer).
4. Add personas only for critical-to-satisfy roles; don't add them by reflex.
